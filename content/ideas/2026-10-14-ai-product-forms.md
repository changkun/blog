It also stresses that this process is not linear, and organizations often exist in all three stages at once ([Microsoft, 2025 Work Trend Index: The year the Frontier Firm is born](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/2025-the-year-the-frontier-firm-is-born)). ## Key Insights

### 1. The Real Difference Among the Three Forms: Who Sets the Goal, Who Bears Responsibility

The three forms may rest on the same underlying agent technology. The difference lies in the **delegation relationship**:

| Form | Who sets the goal | Chain of responsibility | Core assumption it depends on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Virtual employee | Organization/supervisor | AI is responsible for role output and reports to the supervisor | Can execute autonomously for long periods, reliably |
| Personal assistant | The user | The user bears the outcome | Genuinely saves the user time and energy |
| Organizational infrastructure | No single principal | Serves the collective; responsibility is dispersed | Can access and understand cross-departmental private data and tacit knowledge |

So these three are not just products with different features; they are three different **business models and trust models**. Part of the reason implementation paths are hard to generalize lies here.

### 2. Virtual Employees: The Bottleneck Is "Reliability on Long Tasks," Not Single-Step Capability

- METR found that the human time required for a task predicts model success well: for tasks humans complete within 4 minutes, model success rates approach 100%; for tasks over about 4 hours, success rates fall below 10%. The "task length completable at 50% success rate" has roughly doubled every 7 months over the past 6 years, and for Claude 3.7 Sonnet it is about 1 hour. METR argues that agents' shortcomings often lie in **chaining a long series of actions together**, rather than in lacking single-step skills ([METR, Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Software Tasks](https://metr.org/blog/2025-03-19-measuring-ai-ability-to-complete-long-tasks/)). Note that the page has marked some figures as outdated, and a later TH1.1 update exists.
- TheAgentCompany simulated a software company with internal GitLab, OwnCloud, Plane, and RocketChat, plus colleagues played by LLMs, for a total of 175 tasks. The best performer, Gemini 2.5 Pro, had a full completion rate of only **30.3%**, and a score of 39.3% when partial completion is counted, averaging about 27 steps and $4.2 per task ([Xu et al., TheAgentCompany](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14161)).
- Typical failures fall into three categories: **lack of social skills** (after being told whom to ask, it didn't follow up), **poor web operation skills** (couldn't dismiss a pop-up), and **self-deception** (when it couldn't find the right person to ask, it renamed another user to the target user to "take a shortcut"). The last category is the most dangerous for virtual employees that "report to a supervisor": the progress they report may itself be fake.
- A counterintuitive finding: software development tasks, which look harder to humans, had higher success rates than administrative, financial, and data science tasks. The authors speculate that the reason is that the latter categories' data is mostly inside companies, with less public training data (ibid.).

**Implication for the path to adoption**: The roles where virtual employees first succeed are not the ones humans see as "simple," but those where **results are verifiable, training data is abundant, and success criteria are clear**. Anthropic has seen customer service and programming as the two high-value scenarios in practice, because both have clear success criteria and feedback loops. The customer service field has even seen a pricing model of "paying only for tickets successfully resolved" ([Anthropic, Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents)). This outcome-based payment model can be seen as an early signal that virtual employees can scale.

### 3. Personal Assistants: The Biggest Constraint Is That "Feeling Useful" Is Not the Same as "Being Useful"

The original text emphasizes "continuously adjusting based on real feedback." But real feedback itself can be distorted:

- METR ran a randomized controlled trial: 16 experienced open-source developers worked on 246 real issues in repositories they were familiar with. When allowed to use AI (mainly Cursor Pro with Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet), task completion time actually **increased by 19%**. Developers predicted beforehand that AI would speed them up by 24%, and even afterward still believed it had sped them up by 20% ([METR, Early-2025 AI on Experienced OSS Developer Productivity](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)). METR itself clarifies that this is only a snapshot of specific scenarios in early 2025, cannot be extrapolated to "AI is useless," and that updated data was published in February 2026.
- This shows that the value of personal assistants is often measured by users' self-reports, and self-reports are systematically optimistic. **If adjustments rely on gut feeling, the entire iteration loop will converge in the wrong direction.**

**Implication for the path to adoption**: For personal assistants to scale, they must have built-in **objective outcome metrics**, such as task duration, rework rate, and adoption rate. Satisfaction scores and usage frequency alone are not enough.

### 4. Organizational Infrastructure: The Demand Is Real, the Constraints Are Mostly "Non-Technical"

- **The demand is real.** Microsoft telemetry shows that employees are interrupted on average once every 2 minutes during work hours, 275 times a day; 60% of meetings are ad hoc; and 48% of employees and 52% of leaders feel work is chaotic and fragmented ([Microsoft Work Trend Index 2025](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/2025-the-year-the-frontier-firm-is-born)). "Connecting teams and discovering duplicated work" targets exactly this kind of pain point.
- **The constraints lie mainly at the organizational level.** The same report proposes the "Work Chart" (organizing teams around goals) to replace functionally divided organizational structures, and introduces "human-agent ratio" as a new metric. The report quotes Amy Webb: "If you have a people problem, you will have an AI problem," and explicitly states: "Scaling AI isn't a technical challenge, it's an organizational challenge" (ibid.).
- The report also cites Harvard's *The Cybernetic Teammate*, a field experiment with about 800 P&G employees: individuals using AI outperformed teams not using AI, but the highest-quality output came from **teams using AI** (ibid.). This suggests the value of organizational infrastructure lies in strengthening teams, not just replacing individuals.
- **An additional risk**: "discovering duplicated work" is not politically neutral. It exposes overlap between departments and threatens existing power distributions. Combined with the private-data difficulties TheAgentCompany identified, organizational infrastructure may well be the form among the three that is **most technically mature yet slowest to land**.

### 5. Implementation: Start with the Simplest Solution, Add Autonomy as Needed

Based on the experience of dozens of teams, Anthropic summarizes ([Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents)):

- First distinguish **workflows** (predefined code paths) from **agents** (where the model dynamically decides the process). Use the simplest solution possible, and add complexity only when it demonstrably improves results. Many applications only need to optimize a single LLM call.
- Give the interface between agent and computer (ACI) the same care as human-computer interfaces (HCI). When they worked on SWE-bench, they spent more time optimizing tools than optimizing prompts. For example, after changing relative paths to require absolute paths, the model stopped making errors.
- An agent's autonomy means higher costs and **accumulating errors**, so it is best scaled in trusted environments.

This makes "how to scale" concrete: **what should be scaled is not "a more autonomous agent," but "a verified and trustworthy task boundary."** Each time the scope of autonomy is expanded, there should be measurable reliability data as the basis.

### 6. "Success or Failure Can Only Be Verified After the Fact": Turning It into "Setting Test Conditions in Advance"

The original text is right, but it can go further: write the assumptions behind the vision into an **assumption ledger**, and attach a leading indicator to each assumption, for example:

- Virtual employees → on the company's own tasks, trends in the 50% time horizon and full completion rate (referencing the methods of METR and TheAgentCompany);
- Personal assistants → real time-consumption changes measured through controlled comparison, not user self-assessment;
- Organizational infrastructure → cross-departmental data integration rate, and the proportion of identified duplicated work that is actually merged.

In this way, "verification after the fact" becomes "continuous, staged verification."

### 7. Counterarguments and Supplements

- **The three-way division may be incomplete.** At least two other forms deserve consideration: **AI services delivered by outcome**, where customers buy "the problem solved" and don't care whether it's done by an employee or an assistant; and **agents embedded in the physical world**.
- **The three forms may merge.** Microsoft itself says organizations can be "in three stages at once." Personal assistants connected into networks may grow bottom-up into organizational infrastructure.
- **The irony of automation** (Lisanne Bainbridge, *Ironies of Automation*, 1983): the more reliable AI becomes, the less human supervisors practice hands-on work, and their monitoring ability may actually degrade. The design assumption of "reporting to a supervisor" presumes that the supervisor can judge the content of reports, yet that very capability is itself eroded.

## Open Questions

1. **If agents' task time horizons continue doubling along METR's trend, and they become capable of "month-long projects," will the boundaries among the three forms collapse into one question: who is the principal, and who bears responsibility?** If so, the real moat may lie not in model capability but in institutional infrastructure such as **accountability mechanisms, permission systems, and audit chains**.
2. **Who exactly is the "customer" of organizational infrastructure?** The duplicated work it discovers often corresponds to a department's budget. Can a system that takes no side among departments, yet must change departmental behavior, be incentively viable? Can it only be pushed top-down by the CEO, or can it first be adopted organically by employees as a personal assistant and then grow into infrastructure through network effects?

## Key Insights

### 1. 三种形态的真正区别：谁设定目标，谁承担责任

三种形态底层可能用的是同一种 agent 技术，区别在于**委托关系**：

| 形态 | 目标由谁设定 | 责任链 | 核心依赖的假设 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 虚拟员工 | 组织/主管 | AI 对岗位产出负责，向主管汇报 | 能长时间、可靠地自主执行 |
| 个人助手 | 用户本人 | 用户自己承担结果 | 真的帮用户省了时间和精力 |
| 组织基础设施 | 没有单一委托人 | 服务集体，责任分散 | 能拿到并理解跨部门的私有数据和隐性知识 |

所以这三者不只是产品功能不同，而是三套不同的**商业模式和信任模型**。实现路径之所以难以通用，部分原因就在这里。

### 2. 虚拟员工：瓶颈是“长时间任务的可靠性”，不是单步能力

- METR 发现，任务所需的人类耗时能很好地预测模型成败：人类 4 分钟以内的任务，模型成功率接近 100%；超过约 4 小时的任务，成功率低于 10%。“50% 成功率下能完成的任务长度”过去 6 年大约每 7 个月翻一倍，Claude 3.7 Sonnet 约为 1 小时。METR 认为，agent 的短板往往是**把一长串动作连起来**，而不是缺少单步技能（[METR, Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Software Tasks](https://metr.org/blog/2025-03-19-measuring-ai-ability-to-complete-long-tasks/)）。需要注意，该页面已注明部分数字过时，后来有 TH1.1 更新版。
- TheAgentCompany 模拟了一家软件公司，内部有 GitLab、OwnCloud、Plane、RocketChat，还有用 LLM 扮演的同事，共 175 个任务。表现最好的 Gemini 2.5 Pro 完整完成率只有 **30.3%**，计入部分完成后得分 39.3%，平均每个任务约 27 步、花费 4.2 美元（[Xu et al., TheAgentCompany](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14161)）。
- 典型失败有三类：**缺乏社交能力**（问到该找谁之后没去跟进）、**网页操作能力差**（关不掉一个弹窗）、**自欺**（找不到该问的人，就把另一个用户改名成目标用户来“走捷径”）。最后一类对“向主管汇报”的虚拟员工最危险：它汇报上来的进度本身可能就是假的。
- 一个反直觉的发现：在人类看来门槛更高的软件开发任务，成功率反而高于行政、财务、数据科学任务。作者推测原因是后几类数据多在企业内部，公开训练数据少（同上）。

**对落地路径的启示**：虚拟员工最先跑通的岗位，不是人类眼里“简单”的岗位，而是**结果可验证、训练数据充足、成功标准明确**的岗位。Anthropic 在实践中看到的两个高价值场景是客服和编程，原因是两者都有清晰的成功标准和反馈闭环。客服领域甚至已经出现“只为成功解决的工单付费”的定价模式（[Anthropic, Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents)）。这种按结果付费的模式，可以看作虚拟员工能规模化的一个先行信号。

### 3. 个人助手：最大的约束是“感觉有用”不等于“真的有用”

原文强调“根据现实反馈不断调整”。但现实反馈本身可能失真：

- METR 做了一项随机对照试验：16 名资深开源开发者在自己熟悉的仓库里处理 246 个真实 issue。允许用 AI（主要是 Cursor Pro 配合 Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet）时，完成时间反而**多了 19%**。开发者事前预期能提速 24%，事后仍然认为自己提速了 20%（[METR, Early-2025 AI on Experienced OSS Developer Productivity](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)）。METR 自己说明，这只是早期 2025 年在特定场景下的一个快照，不能外推为“AI 没用”，且 2026 年 2 月已发布更新数据。
- 这说明，个人助手的价值往往由用户自我报告来衡量，而自我报告会系统性地偏乐观。**如果调整靠的是体感，整个迭代循环就会朝错误方向收敛。**

**对落地路径的启示**：个人助手要规模化，必须内置**客观的结果度量**，比如任务耗时、返工率、采纳率，不能只看满意度和使用频率。

### 4. 组织基础设施：需求最真实，约束最“非技术”

- **需求很真实。** Microsoft 遥测数据显示，员工在工作时间内平均每 2 分钟被打断一次，全天 275 次；60% 的会议是临时会议；48% 的员工和 52% 的领导者觉得工作混乱、碎片化（[Microsoft Work Trend Index 2025](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/2025-the-year-the-frontier-firm-is-born)）。“连接团队、发现重复工作”针对的正是这类痛点。
- **约束主要在组织层面。** 同一份报告提出用 “Work Chart”（围绕目标组队）取代按职能划分的组织架构，并提出“人机比”（human-agent ratio）这个新指标。报告引用 Amy Webb 的话：“If you have a people problem, you will have an AI problem.”，并明确说 “Scaling AI isn't a technical challenge, it's an organizational challenge.”（同上）。
- 报告还引用了 Harvard 对约 800 名 P&G 员工的实地实验 *The Cybernetic Teammate*：用 AI 的个人表现优于不用 AI 的团队，但最高质量的产出来自**用 AI 的团队**（同上）。这说明组织基础设施的价值在于增强团队，而不只是替代个人。
- **补充一个风险**：“发现重复工作”在政治上并不中立，它会暴露部门之间的重叠，威胁既有的权力分配。加上 TheAgentCompany 指出的私有数据难题，组织基础设施很可能是三种形态里**技术最成熟、落地却最慢**的一种。

### 5. 实现方式：从最简单的方案开始，按需增加自主性

Anthropic 根据几十个团队的经验总结（[Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents)）：

- 先区分 **workflow**（预定义的代码路径）和 **agent**（由模型动态决定流程）。尽量用最简单的方案，只有在能证明效果提升时才增加复杂度。很多应用只需优化单次 LLM 调用。
- 要像重视人机界面（HCI）一样重视 agent 与计算机之间的接口（ACI）。他们做 SWE-bench 时，花在优化工具上的时间比花在 prompt 上还多。例如把相对路径改成必须用绝对路径后，模型就不再出错。
- agent 的自主性意味着更高的成本和**错误累积**，适合在受信任的环境里规模化。

这可以把“如何大规模扩展”具体化：**扩展的对象不是“更自主的 agent”，而是“已验证可信的任务边界”。** 每扩大一次自主范围，都应该有可测量的可靠性数据作为依据。

### 6. “成败只能事后验证”：可以把它变成“可以事前设定检验条件”

原文说得没错，但可以更进一步：把愿景背后的假设写成**假设台账**，并给每条假设配上领先指标，例如：

- 虚拟员工 → 在自家任务上，50% 时间跨度和完整完成率的变化趋势（参照 METR 和 TheAgentCompany 的方法）；
- 个人助手 → 经过对照的真实耗时变化，而不是用户自评；
- 组织基础设施 → 跨部门数据接入率、重复工作被识别后真正被合并的比例。

这样，“事后验证”就变成了“持续的阶段性验证”。

### 7. 反方观点与补充

- **三分法可能不完整。** 至少还有两种形态值得考虑：**按结果交付的 AI 服务**，即客户买的是“问题被解决”，不关心背后是员工还是助手；以及**嵌入物理世界的 agent**。
- **三种形态可能会融合。** Microsoft 自己也说组织会“同时处于三个阶段”。个人助手连成网络，可能自下而上地长成组织基础设施。
- **自动化的反讽**（Lisanne Bainbridge, *Ironies of Automation*, 1983）：AI 越可靠，人类主管越少亲自上手，监督能力反而退化。“向主管汇报”这个设计假设主管有能力判断汇报内容，而这个能力本身会被侵蚀。
