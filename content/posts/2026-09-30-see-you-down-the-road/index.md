Dear friends, colleagues, and everyone I have shared a project, a debate, a lunch, a late evening, a weekend, or an experience with:

I am writing to wrap up my thesis at Sixt. As with every thesis, the part that lasts may not be the chapters but the acknowledgments, so let me start there.

My journey with Sixt began four years ago, and I still remember the first day clearly. I arrived in Pullach hours early and sat at the station finishing a paper for a research conference. It was due that day, and I had worked on it for months, including the night before. When time was up, I knew I had to let it go rather than polish it toward perfection. Then I walked into the building, waited to be picked up, and set out to see what would come next. Today I leave as a completely different person, and most of that difference came from all of you. Thank you.

Thank you for giving me the opportunity to begin this journey. Thank you for walking me into this place, for connecting me with the people around it, and for having my back ever since. Thank you for guiding me through how the old pricing systems worked. Thank you for building their replacement with me and taking it live. Thank you for the interesting challenges, and for helping me understand the business motivation behind them. For the fun and laughter, the calm and the hard work, the fights and struggles, the bug reports and fixes, and the on-call nights: thank you all. When I joined, the whole area ran on a legacy stack. Today it is fully modernized.

Thank you for the fantastic collaboration on pricing automation. Thank you for all the fun, sometimes heated, but always constructive discussions. Thank you for the jokes, and for our debates about idiomatic Go. Thank you for the data analysis, and for the investigations we did together. Together we greatly simplified our systems and laid the groundwork for data-driven pricing automation.

Thank you for the inspiring talk that started a new pricing system, and for the trust and help in moving things forward. I still clearly remember the afternoon when we quickly sketched its architecture diagrams. Thank you for one of the most exciting weeks and weekends, when we brought its very first version live within days, and that was before AI. Thank you for scaling it into a truly production-grade system.

Thank you for the opportunities, for building the environment, and for shaping how we apply AI. My trajectory would have been entirely different if we had not debated an architecture on the blackboard a year and a half ago. Thank you for teaming up with such motivation, entrepreneurial pragmatism, and hard work to bring up our AI platform and the ecosystem around it. Thank you for being inspiring sparring partners on the LLM gateway, agent memory, and agent sandboxes. Thank you for volunteering your spare time to polish the platform. Thank you for adopting early and experimenting first, which set things up for everyone who came after. Thank you for making our systems talk to each other. Thank you for debating all sorts of security concerns around AI with me, and for setting up and evaluating the guardrails. Thank you for the great discussions around Go, and for contributing to the wider Go community. Thank you for the journey of transforming customer service. Thank you for the legendary infrastructure support through all these years, and for the long nights we spent together. Thanks to all of this effort, our AI stack is where it is today.

Thank you for the support, the brainstorming, the discussions, and the building that made Python a first-class citizen and grew a Python community. Thank you for polishing, contributing to, and helping scale the automated code review. Without you, these community-driven things would not have been adopted so widely.

And thank you to everyone not mentioned here, as the space is too narrow to contain.

Thank you all, for the agency, the energy, the understanding, and the listening. We fought hard and then settled completely. Together we reshaped our surroundings for the better, and together we created value and meaning.

There is one spirit I want to share. I watched many areas that looked stable get rebuilt within short periods. The people who acted first were not the most certain ones; they were the ones willing to be wrong in public. So try to be creative before being correct, and stay open-minded when a prior no longer holds. Most of what we build here will be replaced, and some of it already has been, by us. Before Sixt, I spent years with existentialism and nihilism and reached a simple belief, and the years since have confirmed it: there may be no meaning waiting for us, but we still choose to create, therefore meaning exists. So push things forward where you stand.

The beauty of being human is that we can adapt. Identity keeps shifting as long as we keep looking for new things to do. Hence the one idea I keep returning to: if we don't do it now, we may never do it at all. So pursue what we truly want, tune out the noise, work hard, and look after ourselves and the people we love along the way.

I loved my time here. It shaped me, and I will carry it with me.

I will close with Bruce Lee's rendering of a much older Chinese idea about water, 上善若水:

> Empty your mind.\
> Be formless, shapeless, like water.\
> You put water into a cup, it becomes the cup.\
> You put water into a bottle, it becomes the bottle.\
> You put it into a teapot, it becomes the teapot.\
> Now, water can flow, or it can crash.\
> Be water, my friend.

We will see each other down the road,\
